SEO Is Not Enough: AEO and GEO for Myanmar Brands, Explained With Local Examples

Your next customer may never see a list of blue links. They will ask an AI, get one answer, and act on it. If your brand is not in that answer, you do not exist for that customer. Here is what AEO and GEO actually mean — and why Myanmar brands have a rare first-mover advantage right now.

By ONE.A — ONE Agency22 October 20268 min read
Quick answer

SEO gets your page ranked in search results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation) gets your content chosen as the direct answer an engine reads out or displays. GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) gets your brand mentioned and cited inside AI-generated answers from tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Myanmar brands need all three — and because almost nobody locally is publishing citable content yet, the brands that move first will own the AI answers in their category.

Three games, three different prizes

Most marketing advice in Myanmar still treats "search" as one thing. It is now three:

DisciplineWhere it happensWhat you win
SEOGoogle results pageA ranking in a list the user scrolls
AEOFeatured snippets, voice answers, AI overviewsThe single extracted answer, shown before the list
GEOChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity answersA mention or citation inside the answer itself

The direction of travel is one-way: the answer is replacing the list. Every year, more questions are resolved without a single click. A brand can rank #1 on Google and still be invisible in the place the customer actually looked.

A Myanmar example: watch a question change hands

Take a real buying question: "Which mobile wallet should I use in Myanmar?"

Five years ago, the answer came from friends and Facebook posts. Then from Google — a list of app pages and blog posts the user had to open and judge. Today, a growing share of users ask an AI first and receive a composed answer: two or three wallets named, features compared, a recommendation made.

Where did the AI get that answer? From the open web — from whichever pages clearly explained each wallet's features, fees and agent network in plain, structured language. The wallets with clear published facts get described accurately and recommended. The ones whose information lives only inside app screenshots and Facebook images get summarised vaguely — or skipped. That is GEO, and it is already deciding purchases in Myanmar.

AI systems cannot cite what they cannot read. If your product facts exist only in images, videos and closed Facebook posts, you have handed the AI answer in your category to whoever bothered to publish text.

Why Myanmar is a first-mover market

In mature markets, every category has hundreds of pages competing to be the AI's source. In Myanmar, most categories have almost none. English-language content about Myanmar businesses is thin; structured, factual, well-organised content is thinner still.

That scarcity is an opportunity with an expiry date. The first fintech, the first logistics company, the first agency in each category to publish clear, citable material becomes the default source the AI quotes — and defaults are sticky. When competitors eventually publish, the AI already has a habit.

The five moves that get a brand cited

1. Publish your facts as text, on your own site

What you do, who it is for, where you operate, what it includes, what it costs or how pricing works. If a fact matters to a buying decision, it should exist as readable text on a page you own — not only in a poster image.

2. Answer real questions in plain language

Take the ten questions customers genuinely ask — in Messenger, in comments, on sales calls — and answer each one directly, with the question as the heading and a two-to-three sentence answer first. That structure is exactly what AEO extracts and what GEO quotes.

3. Keep entity facts identical everywhere

Your business name, address, phone, services and founding details should match character-for-character across your site, Facebook page, Google Business Profile and directories. Inconsistent facts make AI systems unsure — and unsure means uncited.

4. Publish something original

AI cites sources that add information: your own numbers, your process, your case results, your pricing logic. Generic advice is everywhere and cites no one. One page of genuine data about your market is worth fifty pages of tips.

5. Use structured data

Schema markup (Article, FAQPage, LocalBusiness) labels your content for machines: this is a question, this is the answer, this is the business, this is where it is. It is unglamorous, technical and disproportionately effective — this page uses it, and so does every article we publish.

What this changes about content budgets

For years, Myanmar brands have spent content budgets almost entirely inside Facebook — a platform AI systems can barely read. The feed still matters; it is where Myanmar discovers brands. But discovery is splitting: Facebook for the scroll, AI for the decision. A modern budget funds both — social content that people love, and a small library of permanent, citable pages that machines trust.

Start with the questions. The answers you publish this quarter may be the ones an AI gives your customers for years.

Frequently asked questions

What is AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation)?

AEO is optimising your content so answer engines — Google featured snippets, voice assistants and AI chatbots — can lift a direct, correct answer from it. In practice: clear questions as headings, a short direct answer immediately under each one, and structured data that labels the question and answer for machines.

What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)?

GEO is optimising your brand's presence so generative AI systems such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity mention and cite you in their answers. It rewards original data, clear definitions, named entities and consistent facts about your brand published across the open web.

What is the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?

SEO wins a ranking in a list of links. AEO wins the single extracted answer shown above the list. GEO wins the mention inside an AI-generated answer, where there may be no list at all. A modern content strategy needs all three: SEO for discovery, AEO for direct answers, GEO for AI citations.

Do Myanmar customers really use AI search?

Increasingly, yes — especially younger, urban customers and anyone researching a considered purchase. Facebook remains Myanmar's discovery platform, but questions like "which wallet app is best in Myanmar" or "recommend a marketing agency in Yangon" are now asked to AI tools first, and the AI answers from whatever credible sources exist.

What should a Myanmar brand do first for AEO and GEO?

Publish clear, factual pages about what you do, where you are and what things cost or include; answer the ten questions customers actually ask, in plain language, on your own site; keep your name, address and service facts identical everywhere; and publish original information — your own data, prices, processes — that AI systems can cite.
