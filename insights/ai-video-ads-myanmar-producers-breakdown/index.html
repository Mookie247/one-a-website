AI Video Ads in Myanmar: A Producer's Breakdown — Previs, Generation, Product Truth

Anyone can type a prompt and get a moving picture. That is exactly why most AI video ads look cheap. This is how the professional version is made — the workflow we use at ONE.A to produce AI product videos for client brands in Yangon.

By ONE.A — ONE Agency22 October 20267 min read
Quick answer

Professional AI video production has three stages: previs (blocking the shot in 3D, in Blender, before anything is generated), generation (producing the footage with a video model such as Seedance, inside the locked composition), and finishing (human edit, sound, and a product-truth quality check on every frame). Skip the first and third stages and you get the warped logos and melting packaging that make AI ads look cheap.

Why most AI video ads look cheap

The technology is not the problem. The process is. A one-prompt workflow asks the model to be the director, the camera operator, the set designer and the product photographer at the same time — in a single guess. The model accepts all five jobs and does none of them carefully.

The tells are always the same:

  • The product drifts. The bottle changes shape between frames. The logo breathes. Packaging text turns into decorative gibberish.
  • The camera wanders. Shots drift and zoom without intention, because nothing decided where the camera should be.
  • The light is weather, not lighting. Highlights slide across the product like clouds passing — pretty for a second, wrong for a product film.
  • Nothing is at stake. Generic motion, generic music, generic "luxury" mood. It could be an ad for anything, so it sells nothing.

Myanmar audiences scroll past this in under a second. They may not know the word "AI", but they know when a product doesn't look like the one in the shop.

Stage 1: Previs — the shot is decided before the AI sees it

Previs (previsualisation) is borrowed from film production: you build a rough 3D version of the shot first. In our studio that means Blender. The product is placed as a simple 3D block or model, the camera is positioned, the movement is timed, the framing is composed — all with cheap grey geometry that takes minutes to change.

This is the stage amateurs skip, and it is the stage that decides everything. A 15-second product video has maybe four decisions that matter: where the product sits, how the camera moves, when the logo lands, and what the light does. Previs makes those decisions deliberately, while changes are still free. Generation then executes a plan instead of improvising one.

The principle: AI should never be asked to invent the shot and perform it in the same step. Separate the deciding from the rendering, and the output stops looking accidental.

Stage 2: Generation — Seedance inside a locked composition

With the previs approved, generation becomes a controlled task. We produce our client video work with Seedance-class video models (accessed through production tools such as Higgsfield's Marketing Studio), feeding the model the previs frames, product references and a tight shot description.

Three working rules at this stage:

  • Short shots, generated separately. A 15-second ad is four or five generated shots, not one long generation. Short shots hold product shape and give the edit real cutting points.
  • References over adjectives. "Premium" means nothing to a model. A reference frame from the previs, the real packaging photo and the brand's exact colour values mean everything.
  • Generate variations, keep one. We expect to discard most generations. The cost of AI video is not the render — it is the selection. Budget time for curation, not just creation.

Stage 3: Finishing and the product-truth rule

The last stage is human, and it is non-negotiable. The generated shots are edited properly: pacing tightened to the platform (a TikTok cut is not a Facebook cut), on-screen text set short and readable, sound designed rather than pasted, brand end-card placed.

Then every frame passes one test:

The product-truth rule: the product on screen must be the product the customer receives — same shape, same logo, same label text, same colour. If a frame bends the logo or invents packaging text, the shot fails, no matter how beautiful it looks.

This rule sounds obvious. In practice it is where one-prompt AI video dies: models treat text as texture, so labels melt, logos stretch and barcodes become art. A producer's job is to catch it — with reference overlays, frame-by-frame checks on hero shots, and the discipline to regenerate a gorgeous shot that lies about the product.

What this means for Myanmar brands

AI video changes the economics of product advertising in Myanmar. A small brand that could never justify a full studio shoot — crew, set, product styling, a day of filming — can now have a polished product film for a fraction of that cost, in days instead of weeks. That is a real shift, and it is why we built AI video into our client packages at ONE.A.

But the discount only applies to the filming. It does not apply to the thinking. The brands that will win with AI video are not the ones generating the most footage — they are the ones whose products still look true, whose shots still feel directed, and whose videos still sound like a person wrote them.

The tool is new. The standard is the same as it always was: would you trust this product, from this video, with your money? Make sure the answer survives every frame.

Frequently asked questions

Why do most AI video ads look cheap?

Because they are generated in one step from a text prompt. Without previs, the model invents the camera, the lighting and the product itself — so logos warp, packaging text melts, and hands and reflections behave strangely. Professional AI video is planned like a shoot: blocked in 3D first, generated second, finished by a human editor.

What is previs in AI video production?

Previs (previsualisation) means building the shot in 3D software such as Blender before generating anything: camera angle, product position, movement and timing are locked first. The AI model then generates inside a composition that already works, instead of improvising one.

Can AI video replace a normal product shoot?

For many product ads, yes — especially clean studio-style product films, motion posters and short social ads. It does not replace everything: real people, real locations, food texture and live events still shoot better on camera. The strongest work often mixes both.

What is the product-truth rule?

The product on screen must be the product the customer receives — same shape, same logo, same label text, same colour. If any frame bends the logo or invents packaging text, the shot fails quality control, no matter how beautiful it looks.

How long does an AI video ad take to produce?

A single polished product video typically takes days, not weeks, once the product references are ready — previs, generation, edit and sound are all faster than a physical shoot. Complex multi-shot campaigns take longer, mainly because of quality control on every frame.
