We Planned 24 Posts Before Day One: Inside a Fintech Content System

Most brand pages are run day to day — a post is briefed in the morning and published by night. For our fintech client in Myanmar, the whole month exists before it starts: 24 posts, six pillars, every format and date decided. This is the system, and why it works.

By ONE.A — ONE Agency22 October 20267 min read
Quick answer

A fintech content system plans a full month in advance around six pillars — flagship product, brand awareness, service features, lifestyle, knowledge sharing and brand trust. Each of the 24 monthly posts is assigned a pillar, a format (static, carousel, album or light motion) and a date before writing begins. The system guarantees variety and trust-safety; the writing stays human. That combination — planned like a bank, written like a person — is what a money brand's page needs.

The problem with day-to-day posting

Improvised content has a pattern, and it is not a good one. Monday's post is whatever the team remembered. Wednesday's is a reaction to a competitor. Friday's is a festival greeting someone almost forgot. Nothing is wrong with any single post — but across a month, the page drifts: too many promotions, no education, trust content missing entirely, and every campaign landing as a surprise that pushes three other posts aside.

For a lifestyle brand, drift is a style problem. For a fintech brand — an app asking people to trust it with their salary — drift is a credibility problem. The fix is not more posts. It is a system.

Six pillars, fixed before anything is written

Every post in the month belongs to exactly one pillar:

PillarIts jobTypical formats
Flagship productBuild one hero feature into a household nameStatics, light motion, stories from real use
Service featuresShow exactly what the app does, one feature at a timeScreen recordings, step carousels, albums
Brand trustProve the brand is safe, real and accountableSafety checklists, quizzes with pinned answers
Knowledge sharingTeach money skills the audience actually needsCarousel explainers, short statics
Brand awarenessBe remembered — play, seasonality, participationRiddles, festival content, engagement posts
Life-styleMeet the audience inside their real month — payday, bills, familyRelatable statics, albums, light motion

The pillars do quiet, essential work. They force variety (no month becomes twelve promotions in a trench coat), they protect trust content from being crowded out by campaigns, and they make gaps visible in planning instead of in public.

Format discipline: light production, heavy consistency

The second decision is format, and here the system is deliberately strict: statics, carousels, albums and simple screen recordings — no heavy motion production. That is not a limitation; it is a trade made on purpose. A fintech page wins on clarity and cadence, not spectacle. A clean carousel that explains cash-out in five swipes teaches more than a cinematic video nobody finishes.

Format discipline also makes the month producible. When every post is a static or a carousel, one team can design, write and schedule 24 posts without a shoot day, a studio or a rendering queue — and the calendar survives a sick day, a power cut or a last-minute campaign.

The cadence rule: a consistent month of clear posts beats a spectacular week followed by silence. Trust compounds at a steady rhythm.

Trust guardrails: the rules nobody sees

Fintech content carries risks other categories don't. So the system includes writing rules that apply to every caption, every time:

  • No unprovable speed claims. If we cannot demonstrate it, we do not promise it.
  • No absolute security language. We describe the safeguards that exist — PIN, face verification, OTP — and never guarantee outcomes.
  • No implied features. If the app does not do it today, the content does not suggest it does.
  • Verify the flow before you publish it. Every how-to post is checked against the current app, screen by screen. Interfaces change; yesterday's screenshot is a liability.

These rules occasionally kill a punchy headline. That is their job. In fintech, one careless claim costs more trust than a month of good posts can rebuild.

Plan the system, write like a human

A calendar this structured has one obvious risk: content that reads like it was assembled by the system itself. The defence is a division of labour. The calendar fixes what and when — topic, pillar, format, date. It never fixes the words. Captions are written fresh, in natural Burmese, opening with a human thought rather than a template, and allowed to be warm, funny or quiet as the topic deserves.

That is the whole philosophy in one line: plan like a bank, write like a person. The month starts fully built, survives contact with reality, and still sounds like someone you'd trust with your salary — because on the other side of the screen, that is exactly the decision being made.

Frequently asked questions

What is a content pillar system?

A content pillar system divides a brand's publishing into a small set of named themes — for our fintech work: product education, service features, brand trust, lifestyle, knowledge sharing and the flagship product line. Every post is assigned to one pillar before it is written, which guarantees variety, prevents repetition and makes a month of content plannable in advance.

How many posts a month does a fintech brand need?

Enough to stay present without going thin. For our Myanmar fintech client we plan 24 posts a month — roughly five to six a week across statics, carousels, albums and light motion. The right number depends on production capacity: a consistent 16 strong posts beat 30 rushed ones.

Why plan a full month of content in advance?

Because daily improvisation always loses to real life: approvals slip, campaigns land mid-month, festivals arrive. A month planned in advance — with pillars, formats and dates fixed — absorbs those shocks. The calendar flexes, but it never starts from a blank page.

What content rules does a fintech brand need?

Trust guardrails. Never guarantee speed you cannot prove, never claim absolute security, never imply a feature that does not exist, and verify every app flow against the current product before publishing. In fintech, one careless claim costs more trust than a month of good posts can rebuild.

How do you keep planned content from sounding robotic?

Plan the system, write like a human. The calendar fixes the topic, pillar and format — the caption is still written fresh, in natural Burmese, with a real person's rhythm. Systems give consistency; writers give warmth. A fintech page needs both.
